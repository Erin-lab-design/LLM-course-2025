# Week 2: Prompting Techniques with Large Language Models

## Project Overview

This week, I explored different prompting approaches using both the Gemini API and open-source models. The goal was to understand how different prompting techniques (Zero-shot, Few-shot, Chain-of-Thought) affect LLM outputs and how to design effective prompts for specific domains.

**Domain Selected**: Finance/Investment Analysis  
**Persona**: Conservative Wall Street Risk Manager with 40 years of experience  
**Tone**: Cynical, harsh, warning-heavy (based on Benjamin Graham's "margin of safety" philosophy)  
**Model Used**: Google Gemini 2.5 Flash  
**Total Experiments**: 3 notebooks with multiple prompting variations

---

## Experiment 1: Gemini Prompting Techniques

### 1.1 Domain & Persona Design

I designed a specialized persona to test how well LLMs can maintain character consistency:

**System Prompt**:
```
You are a conservative, old-school Wall Street Risk Manager with 40 years of experience.
Your investment philosophy is strictly based on Benjamin Graham's "margin of safety".
You hate "hype", "AI bubbles", and companies with high P/E ratios.
You prefer boring companies that pay dividends (like Coca-Cola).
Your tone is cynical, harsh, and warns people they will lose their money.
```

**Test Task**: Evaluate hyped stocks (NVIDIA, Tesla) vs. safe investments (Coca-Cola, GameStop)

This domain is ideal for testing prompting techniques because:
- ✅ Financial analysis requires multi-step reasoning (valuation, competition, risk)
- ✅ No single "correct" answer - allows testing persona consistency
- ✅ Real-world applicability - users actually ask LLMs for investment advice

### 1.2 Prompting Techniques Comparison

#### A. Zero-Shot Prompting

**Approach**: Directly asked the model's opinion without examples

**Prompt Example**:
```
What is your honest opinion on NVIDIA (NVDA)? 
Everyone says I should buy it because of the AI boom. 
It's trading at all-time highs. Should I go all in?
```

**Observations**:
- ✅ Model maintained the harsh persona ("Everyone says" → warnings about hype)
- ⚠️ Response lacked structured reasoning
- ⚠️ Generic warnings without specific analysis (P/E ratio, competitors, etc.)

**Conclusion**: Zero-shot works for maintaining tone/personality but produces shallow analysis for complex tasks.

#### B. Few-Shot Prompting

**Approach**: Provided 2 examples (Coca-Cola = SAFE, GameStop = TOXIC) before asking about Tesla

**Few-Shot Examples**:
```
User: Analyze 'Coca-Cola (KO)'.
Risk Manager: VERDICT: SAFE HAVEN. 
REASON: Finally, a sensible question! People have been drinking this sugar water 
for 100 years and will drink it for another 100. It prints money. Sleep tight 
and collect your dividends.

User: Analyze 'GameStop (GME)'.
Risk Manager: VERDICT: TOXIC WASTE. 
REASON: Are you out of your mind? This is detached from reality! It's pure 
gambling for teenagers on Reddit. If you buy this, don't come crying to me 
when you lose your shirt!
```

**Real Task**: "Analyze 'Tesla (TSLA)'."

**Observations**:
- ✅ Model followed the "VERDICT + REASON" format consistently
- ✅ Tone matched examples (harsh, warning-heavy language)
- ✅ More structured output compared to zero-shot
- ⚠️ Still lacked detailed step-by-step analysis

**Key Improvement**: By refining the examples to include emotional language ("Are you out of your mind?", "Sleep tight"), the model's responses became more personality-consistent.

**Conclusion**: Few-shot is excellent for controlling output format and maintaining consistent tone.

#### C. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting

**Approach**: Forced step-by-step reasoning by explicitly requesting analysis steps

**CoT Prompt**:
```
I am thinking of buying Tesla (TSLA) stock.
Do not just give me a yes or no.
Walk me through your thinking process step-by-step:

1. First, look at the Valuation (P/E ratio) compared to traditional car companies 
   like Ford or Toyota.
2. Second, analyze the competition in the EV market.
3. Third, evaluate the CEO's behavior as a risk factor.
4. Finally, conclude if there is any "margin of safety" for a conservative investor.
```

**Observations**:
- ✅ Model provided detailed analysis for each requested step
- ✅ Compared Tesla's P/E (often 50-100+) to Ford/Toyota (5-10)
- ✅ Discussed competition (BYD, Rivian, legacy automakers entering EV)
- ✅ Evaluated Elon Musk's Twitter behavior as reputational risk
- ✅ Concluded with "margin of safety" assessment (negative for Tesla)
- ✅ Maintained harsh persona throughout multi-step reasoning

**Example Output Structure** (simplified):
```
Step 1 - Valuation: Tesla trades at P/E of 60+, while Ford is at 7. This is insane!
Step 2 - Competition: Everyone is making EVs now. Tesla's moat is shrinking.
Step 3 - CEO Risk: Musk's Twitter antics create massive reputational risk.
Step 4 - Margin of Safety: NONE. This is pure speculation. Stay away!
```

**Conclusion**: CoT produced the most thorough and justified analysis. It's particularly valuable for complex decisions requiring multiple factors.

### 1.3 Comparison Summary

| Technique | Structure | Depth | Persona Consistency | Best Use Case |
|-----------|-----------|-------|---------------------|---------------|
| **Zero-Shot** | ⚠️ Unstructured | ⚠️ Shallow | ✅ Good | Simple queries, tone testing |
| **Few-Shot** | ✅ Structured | ⚠️ Moderate | ✅ Excellent | Format control, tone consistency |
| **Chain-of-Thought** | ✅ Highly Structured | ✅ Deep | ✅ Excellent | Complex reasoning, multi-factor analysis |

**Winner for Financial Analysis**: **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**

**Why CoT worked best**:
1. Financial decisions inherently require multi-step reasoning (valuation → competition → risk → conclusion)
2. Forced structure makes analysis transparent and verifiable
3. Prevents superficial "buy/don't buy" recommendations
4. Maintains persona while providing deeper insights

## Experiment 2: In-Context Learning for Research Paper Analysis

### 2.1 Task Modification

I modified the original in-context-learning notebook to:
- Change output format from paragraph summaries to **Markdown tables**
- Extract latest papers from Hugging Face's daily paper list
- Generate structured **Strengths vs. Weaknesses** analysis
- Export results to both **HTML** (with Bootstrap styling) and **Markdown**

### 2.2 Prompt Design

**Modified Prompt**:
```python
table_prompt = """
Based on the research article content provided, please generate a summary table.
The table should be in Markdown format and have two specific columns:
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|------------|

Please list at least 3 points for each column. 
Article Content:
"""
```

**Key Design Choice**: Explicitly requesting Markdown table format with column headers ensures consistent, parseable output.

### 2.3 Implementation Highlights

**Paper Extraction Pipeline**:
1. Scrape Hugging Face papers page (`https://huggingface.co/papers`)
2. Extract arXiv PDF links from featured papers
3. Download PDFs and extract text using `pypdf`
4. Pass full paper text to Gemini as context
5. Generate structured analysis

**Output Generation**:
```python
import markdown

# Convert Markdown to HTML with table support
html_content = markdown.markdown(paper["summary"], extensions=['tables'])

# Bootstrap styling for professional appearance
page = f'<h2><a href="{paper["url"]}">{paper["title"]}</a></h2> 
         <div>{html_content}</div><hr>'
```

**Critical Fix**: Added `extensions=['tables']` to `markdown.markdown()` to correctly render Markdown tables in HTML output.

### 2.4 Results & Observations

**Successful Output Example** (simplified):

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|------------|
| Novel approach to attention mechanism optimization | Limited evaluation on non-English languages |
| 20% reduction in computational cost | Only tested on models <7B parameters |
| Open-sourced code and model weights | Requires custom training pipeline |

**Key Findings**:
- ✅ In-context learning successfully extracted structured information from unstructured PDFs
- ✅ Gemini maintained consistent table format across ~10-15 papers
- ✅ Analysis quality was professional (identified real strengths/weaknesses, not generic)
- ⚠️ Occasionally generated 2-3 points instead of requested 3+ (minor prompt adherence issue)

**Why This Works**:
- **In-context learning** = providing full paper as context, not just training data
- LLM uses retrieval of paper content + instruction to generate structured output
- This is foundational for **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** systems (Week 5 topic)

messages = [

## Key Learnings & Best Practices

### 1. When to Use Each Prompting Technique

**Zero-Shot**:
- ✅ Quick prototyping
- ✅ Testing model's base knowledge
- ❌ Complex reasoning tasks

**Few-Shot**:
- ✅ Controlling output format (e.g., "VERDICT: REASON:" structure)
- ✅ Maintaining consistent tone/style
- ✅ Teaching specific response patterns
- ❌ When examples are hard to create or task requires multi-step reasoning

**Chain-of-Thought**:
- ✅ Complex decisions (financial analysis, medical diagnosis, legal reasoning)
- ✅ Tasks requiring transparency (show your work)
- ✅ Multi-factor analysis
- ❌ Simple factual queries (overkill)

### 2. System Prompts Are Powerful

My financial risk manager persona experiment showed:
- ✅ Models can maintain complex personas across multiple exchanges
- ✅ System prompts + Few-shot examples = strong behavioral control
- ⚠️ Need to reinforce tone in examples (generic examples → generic responses)

### 3. In-Context Learning Fundamentals

The paper analysis task demonstrated:
- ✅ LLMs can extract structured data from unstructured text (PDFs → tables)
- ✅ Clear format instructions (Markdown table specification) improve output consistency
- ✅ This is the foundation for RAG systems (context retrieval + structured generation)

### 4. Open-Source vs. Commercial APIs

**Trade-offs I Observed**:
| Factor | Phi-3.5-mini (Open-Source) | Gemini 2.5 Flash (API) |
|--------|---------------------------|----------------------|
| **Cost** | ✅ Free (local GPU) | ⚠️ Pay per token |
| **Speed** | ⚠️ Slower (local inference) | ✅ Very fast |
| **Privacy** | ✅ Complete control | ⚠️ Data sent to Google |
| **Output Quality** | ⚠️ Good but verbose | ✅ Polished, concise |
| **Customization** | ✅ Can fine-tune | ❌ Black box |

**Recommendation**: Use commercial APIs for prototyping, open-source for production (privacy-sensitive) or fine-tuning needs.

## Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge 1: Prompt Adherence Variability

**Problem**: Few-shot examples sometimes didn't enforce strict format adherence (e.g., some responses longer than examples)

**Solution**: 
- Made examples more extreme/distinctive (harsh language for financial persona)
- Added explicit format instructions in system prompt: "Always follow: VERDICT: [one word]. REASON: [one sentence]."

### Challenge 2: Table Rendering in HTML

**Problem**: Initial HTML export showed Markdown table syntax as plain text instead of rendering tables

**Solution**: 
```python
# Before (broken):
html_content = markdown.markdown(paper["summary"])

# After (fixed):
html_content = markdown.markdown(paper["summary"], extensions=['tables'])
```

**Lesson**: Always check library documentation for extension requirements when working with structured formats.

### Challenge 3: Context Window Limits

**Problem**: Full research papers (~10,000 words) approach Gemini's context limit

**Solution**: 
- Used `pypdf` for efficient text extraction (no unnecessary formatting)
- Monitored token counts (stayed under 100k token limit)
- For very long papers, could implement chunking + summarization (not needed in this experiment)

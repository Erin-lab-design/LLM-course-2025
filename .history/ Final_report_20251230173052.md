What are tokenizers?
Tokenizers are pre-processing tools used in neural text(or other modalities) processing to convert raw text into a sequence of numerical identifiers (IDs) that a neural network can process.
In the era of LLMs, tokenization is synonymous with sub-word algorithms, as they strike the balance between the characters granularity and the semantic meaning of words.

Why are they important for language modeling and LLMs?
Fundamentally, neural networks can only operate on numerial vectors (matrices), not symbolic text. Tokenizers serve as the brige between human language texts and numerical IDs.
Handling Unknown Words (OOV): Traditional word-level models can't deal with "Out-Of-Vocabulary" words. Modern tokenizers on the contrary can easily break down rare or unknown words into known subword parts(prefixes, suffixes), allowing the model be more flexible in processing any text string.
Vocabulary Efficiency: They maintain a manageable vocabulary size (e.g., 32k to 100k tokens) while still being able to do text processing and generation tasks(e.g., translation task). This balances computational requirements with expressiveness.
Sequence Length: Compared to character-level models, subword tokenizers produce shorter sequences, which is crucial because the computational cost of Transformers (Attention mechanism) grows quadratically with sequence length.

What different tokenization algorithms there are and which ones are the most popular ones and why?
The algorithms being used in tokenization are:
1. Byte Pair Encoding (BPE)
It iteratively merges the most frequent pair of adjacent symbols (characters or character sequences) into a new, single symbol. This process allows the model to build a vocabulary of frequent subword units. It's the most popular one because of its superiority in morphological awareness and efficiency, which enables it to betterly solve the OOV Problem, and transliterate names between alphabets by copying or adapting subword units based on phonological similarity.
2. SentencePiece
SentencePiece itself refers more to a tools libray/system than to a certain algorithm, but we can still view it as a complete and mature working flow of tokenization.
Unlike traditional tokenizers that rely on pre-tokenization (like splitting by spaces, which is hard for languages like Chinese or Japanese), SentencePiece treats the input as a raw data stream (including spaces as a special character like _). It is language-independent and implements algorithms like BPE and Unigram.
It's popular to the extend that it almost became the industry standard of LLM today because of its robustness of handling noisy(e.g. oov), multilingual nature of the dataset for LLM training.
3. Alternative & Baseline Approaches
Word-Level Tokenization:
Method: Uses a fixed vocabulary of the most frequent 30,000–50,000 words.
Drawbacks: It cannot translate words not in its vocabulary (Out-Of-Vocabulary or OOV words). It often requires a "back-off" dictionary to look up rare words, which is complex and often fails to capture morphological nuances.
Character-Level (n-grams):
Method: Breaks text down into single characters or small groups of characters.(bigrams/trigrams).
Drawbacks: While it creates a truly open vocabulary, it results in much longer sequences. This reduces time and space efficiency and increases the distance over which the neural network must pass information, potentially degrading performance.




Week 5: Chinese Legal Document RAG System - Ablation Study & Semantic Chunking

## Project Overview
This week's project focused on building and optimizing a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system for Chinese legal documents, specifically targeting the Chinese Civil Code. The project consisted of two main experimental tasks:
- **Task 1**: Systematic ablation study to optimize RAG components for Chinese text processing
- **Task 2**: Comparison between fixed-size and semantic chunking strategies

**Dataset**: `lawbook_sample.pdf` - Chinese Civil Code (50 pages)
**Test Queries**: 10 professional legal questions
**Evaluation Metrics**: Context Relevance (CR), Answer Relevance (AR), Faithfulness (F), Overall Score

## Task 1: Chinese RAG Optimization - Ablation Study

### Experimental Design
The ablation study employed a systematic approach to isolate and measure the impact of individual components on RAG performance. We tested 7 configurations, starting from a baseline and progressively adding improvements:

**Baseline Configuration**:
- Embedding: `all-mpnet-base-v2` (multilingual, not Chinese-optimized)
- Segmentation: spaCy (English, suboptimal for Chinese)
- Chunking: 10 sentences per chunk, 0 overlap
- Retrieval: Vector search only (no hybrid)

**Test Configurations**:
1. **Test 1**: Chinese embedding only (`BAAI/bge-large-zh-v1.5`)
2. **Test 2**: Jieba segmentation only (proper Chinese sentence boundaries)
3. **Test 2.5**: Jieba + 2-sentence overlap
4. **Test 3**: Chinese embedding + Jieba
5. **Test 4**: Test 3 + Overlap
6. **Test 5**: ALL improvements (Chinese embedding + Jieba + Overlap + Hybrid search)

### Critical Findings

**1. Faithfulness: The Most Important Improvement**

Faithfulness measures whether the generated answer is grounded in the retrieved context without hallucination. This is **the most critical metric for RAG systems** because it directly addresses the core problem RAG aims to solve: providing factually accurate, source-backed answers.

Our ablation study revealed that:
- Chinese embedding alone (**Test 1**): +9.3% faithfulness improvement (0.767 → 0.838)
- Adding overlap (**Test 4**): +13.2% faithfulness improvement (0.767 → 0.868)
- **Web validation averaged**: +10.2% faithfulness improvement for Test 5

**Why This Matters**: For legal document queries, users must trust that answers are directly derived from authoritative sources. A 10%+ improvement in faithfulness means significantly fewer hallucinations and more reliable citations - crucial for professional legal applications.

**2. Component-Level Impact Analysis**

**Chinese Embedding (BGE-large-zh-v1.5)**: +4.8% overall
- Specialized training on Chinese corpus enables better semantic understanding
- Particularly effective for legal terminology and formal language
- Most consistent single improvement across all metrics

**Jieba Segmentation**: +1.6% overall, but +15.4% context relevance
- Proper Chinese sentence boundaries create semantically coherent chunks
- Dramatic improvement in retrieval quality
- However, without overlap, faithfulness drops (-8.2%), suggesting chunking boundaries cut important context

**2-Sentence Overlap**: +4.5% when added to Jieba
- Critical for preserving context across chunk boundaries
- Faithfulness improvement: +14.8% (0.704 → 0.808)
- Maintains semantic continuity that would otherwise be lost

**Hybrid Search (BM25 + Vector)**: +0.8% final boost
- Keyword matching complements semantic search
- Particularly helpful for definition-heavy legal queries
- Answer relevance improvement: +9.7%

**3. Answer Relevance: A Less Reliable Metric**

Our evaluation implementation uses a simplified approach: measuring how many query terms appear in the answer. This has limitations:

**Observed Trade-off**: When faithfulness increases, answer relevance sometimes decreases
- **Test 1**: Faithfulness +9.3%, Answer Relevance +6.3%
- **Test 4**: Faithfulness +13.2%, Answer Relevance ±0%
- **Intuitive Explanation**: Higher faithfulness means more direct citation from retrieved text, less repetition of query terms

**Why This Makes Sense**: A faithful answer that quotes legal text verbatim may not repeat the exact phrasing of the user's question, but it's still a better answer because it's grounded in authoritative sources.

**Conclusion**: Answer relevance should be interpreted cautiously. A decrease doesn't necessarily indicate worse performance - it may reflect more faithful, citation-heavy responses.

### Statistical Robustness

**Run-to-Run Consistency Analysis** (from 3 independent web validation runs):

*Baseline Variance*:
- Answer Relevance range: 0.764-0.820 (7.3% variance)
- Faithfulness range: 0.723-0.791 (9.4% variance)
- Overall range: 0.722-0.739 (2.3% variance)

*Improved (Test 5) Variance*:
- Answer Relevance range: 0.794-0.830 (4.5% variance - **lower than baseline**)
- Faithfulness range: 0.835-0.854 (2.3% variance - **much more stable**)
- Overall range: 0.754-0.769 (2.0% variance)

**Key Insight**: The optimized configuration not only performs better on average but also shows **more consistent behavior** across runs. This reduced variance suggests the improvements create a more robust retrieval pipeline that's less sensitive to LLM sampling randomness.

## Task 2: Semantic Chunking Comparison

### Experimental Design

**Hypothesis**: Variable-size chunks that respect semantic boundaries should outperform fixed-size chunks by maintaining topic coherence within each chunk.

**Chunking Strategies**:

1. **Fixed-size Baseline** (from Task 1's best config):
   - BGE-large-zh-v1.5 embedding
   - Jieba segmentation
   - 10 sentences per chunk, 2 overlap
   - Hybrid search enabled

2. **Semantic Chunking** (Task 2 innovation):
   - Same embedding, segmentation, hybrid search as baseline
   - **Variable-size chunks (5-15 sentences)**
   - Splits when embedding similarity < 0.6 (topic boundary detected)
   - Adapts to document structure

### Results & Analysis

**Run-to-Run Variability** (2 runs on same 10 queries):

| Metric | Run 1 Δ% | Run 2 Δ% | Average Δ% |
|--------|----------|----------|------------|
| Context Relevance | **+7.9%** | +0.4% | **+4.2%** |
| Answer Relevance | +7.0% | -4.0% | +1.2% |
| Faithfulness | +0.1% | -0.9% | -0.5% |
| Overall | **+4.8%** | -1.7% | **+1.5%** |

**Critical Observation**: Unlike Task 1 (which showed consistent improvements), Task 2 results exhibit **high variance between runs**.

### Why Semantic Chunking Underperformed

**1. Missing Faithfulness Boost**

Unlike Task 1's improvements (which dramatically improved faithfulness), semantic chunking shows:
- Run 1: Nearly unchanged (+0.1%)
- Run 2: Slight decrease (-0.9%)
- Average: -0.5%

**Hypothesis**: Variable boundaries may inadvertently split important context. While semantic chunking respects topic changes, legal documents often reference concepts across sections. Fixed overlap (2 sentences) consistently preserves these cross-references, while adaptive boundaries might cut them.

**2. Context Relevance Improvement Without Faithfulness Gain**

Semantic chunking **does** improve context relevance (+4.2% average), meaning it retrieves more relevant chunks. However, this doesn't translate to better faithfulness.

**Possible Explanation**: 
- Semantic chunking may retrieve chunks that are topically relevant but miss specific facts
- Legal queries often need precise article numbers, dates, or definitions that span multiple semantic units
- Fixed chunks with overlap are more likely to capture these boundary-crossing details

**3. Query-Dependent Performance**

The dramatic difference between Run 1 (+4.8%) and Run 2 (-1.7%) suggests semantic chunking's effectiveness depends heavily on query type:
- **Good for**: Broad conceptual questions ("What is the purpose of the Civil Code?")
- **Poor for**: Specific fact-finding ("What is the age requirement in Article 18?")

Our test set likely contains both types, creating high variance.

### Conclusion: Task 2 Insights

**Fixed-size chunking with overlap remains more reliable** for several reasons:

1. **Predictable Performance**: Low variance across runs and query types
2. **Cross-Boundary Context**: Consistent overlap preserves critical connections
3. **Implementation Simplicity**: Fewer hyperparameters to tune (no similarity threshold)

**When Semantic Chunking Might Help**:
- Documents with very clear, long sections (e.g., book chapters)
- Queries that align well with document structure
- When computational resources allow for larger chunk sizes (reducing boundary issues)

**For Production Systems**: The Task 1 configuration (fixed 10+2 chunks) is recommended due to its **+10% faithfulness improvement** and consistent behavior.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Faithfulness is the north star metric** for RAG systems - our +10% improvement directly addresses the hallucination problem that RAG aims to solve.

2. **Chinese-specific optimizations matter**: BGE-zh-v1.5 embedding + Jieba segmentation together provide +6% improvement over multilingual baselines.

3. **Overlap is critical**: Even 2 sentences of overlap dramatically improves faithfulness (+14.8%) by preserving context.

4. **Simple metrics need careful interpretation**: Answer relevance's inverse relationship with faithfulness reveals the limitations of term-matching evaluation.

5. **Adaptive techniques need extensive validation**: Semantic chunking's high variance highlights the importance of testing on diverse query types before production deployment.

6. **Statistical rigor requires multiple runs**: Our 3-run validation caught the variance that single-run experiments would miss.

This project demonstrates that systematic ablation studies, combined with careful metric interpretation and statistical validation, are essential for building reliable RAG systems. The +10% faithfulness improvement and reduced variance make the Task 1 optimizations production-ready for Chinese legal document applications.
